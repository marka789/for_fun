A diary — Hong Kong → Telford → Birkenhead → Edinburgh → London → home

Friday 4 – Saturday 5 September — China Eastern

Hong Kong → Shanghai Pudong T1 → London Gatwick. Bags through, same terminal.

The food on the plane was terrific. The transfer in Shanghai was smooth. China Eastern is an amazing airline.

Saturday 5 September — On the way to Telford

Sunny, no clouds. About 13–16°C. Perfect vacation weather.

We landed at Gatwick after that good overnight, and almost immediately got on the wrong train — Thameslink heading toward Bedford instead of Victoria. It was all good. We got off at St Pancras International and walked to Euston. People here walk really fast. One of the suitcase wheels is already a bit broken. Oh well.

The walk is only a few minutes and not pretty: Euston Road, buses, everyone towing cases. Then the 9:10 Avanti to Birmingham New Street, due in at 10:34, and onward to Telford Central.

We made it to the Airbnb. On the tiled kitchen wall: a white plate, two red switches, a plug already in. One switch is labelled cooker. Not the boiler. The cooker.

Lunch at Wildwood in Southwater. The tuna avocado pâté had a nice little blue cheese surprise. The lasagna kinda tasted like borscht. The hard cheese was yum — Sasha’s verdict.

Hickory’s Smokehouse in Priorslee at 7:30. Shrewsbury with Jason and Mia tomorrow — not today.

Saturday evening — supermarkets, a long walk, a hotpot jacket

We fought the jet lag by staying out and walked the entire Telford Central area, in and out of Iceland (frozen pre-prepped food is shockingly cheap here), Aldi, Poundland, then further up to Sainsbury's, where most of the real shopping happened, plus a round at M&S. Fruit, frozen food, salmon, steak — so much cheaper than home. Genuinely excited to cook this trip.

Hickory's Smokehouse was far from the Airbnb and we walked anyway. Maybe a mistake — we ended up crossing roundabouts on foot, which felt a bit dangerous — but it was relaxing all the same, and it got colder as the night came in. We made it safe.

Inside: warm, cabin vibes. We ordered the two-person platter, which was way too much food, and they brought popcorn as a starter — gone in minutes. The brisket was so tender it was Sasha's favourite by far. Mark's favourite was the honey-glazed chicken wings, which tasted like the wings from Loblaws back in Toronto. Uber home — late, cold, sleepy.

Back at the Airbnb we ran the washing machine empty before trusting it with a real load, then fought the portable dryer, whose cord was too short to reach anywhere useful. The fix: Mark's belt looped through the door hook as an extra hanging point, so Sasha's jacket — which smelled like hotpot — could finally dry. Tidied up, chilled, bed.

London base — decided from Telford

We cancelled the Darwin Street Airbnb and took Chelsea Cloisters on Sloane Avenue. Check-in at three. If we arrive early: bags down, walk to Harrods.

Sunday 6 September — Shrewsbury with Jason & Mia

Up around 8:30, sleepy and groggy — woke a few times overnight from rain and jet lag.

Breakfast was Quaker Oats, surprisingly good, plus washed grapes and blueberries to start the day off right. Then Jason and Mia came to pick us up in their Mini Cooper. We drove 30–40 minutes over to Shrewsbury, a really nice and cute town in Shropshire — Charles Darwin used to live here.

We grabbed a coffee (still have the plastic cup) and made our way along the riverside. There was a marathon on, so it was busy, but with Maple — their 5-year-old cockapoo — along for the walk, it was very relaxing. Jason, Mia, and Maple were like a cute old British couple doing their own thing, and we just trailed behind them, so it never felt awkward. They brought us to The Dingle, the botanical garden in Shrewsbury that started in 1875 — really nice and peaceful. Maple wanted to jump into the pond to play with the ducks.

Lunch was bagels, subs, and toasties from a random store. The smoked salmon bagel was sooo good, so was the roast beef sub — super soft sourdough baguette, and the bagel itself soft but chewy. We then walked around, popping in and out of cute little stores, before sitting down for tea at Ginger and Co, a cute neighbourhood coffee shop and cafe. Just chatted — very relaxing, and the weather was really good.

Then it was back to Sainsbury's for another round of groceries, just with Jason this time — Mia stayed home with Maple. Jason and Mia drove us back to the Airbnb, getting in around 4:15pm.

We made a quick dash to Iceland for more frozen food, since we didn't have enough for a few more meals — it started raining just as we went, but we made it in fast enough to only get a bit wet. Headed home a different, more direct route, similar to the one from the bus stop back to the Airbnb.

Dinner was a proper cook-in: steak, broccolini, cherry tomatoes, leftover BBQ from the night before, and some frozen pasta from Iceland. Very yummy — what a vibe. Gobbled it down. The kitchen tap is a bit weird, so washing up became a two-person job: Sasha held her plastic coffee cup to splash water over the dishes while Mark soaped everything up. All clean in the end.

Showered, then set up a new extension cord to dry clothes in the bedroom — a big upgrade from the belt-on-the-door-hook trick. Sat around waiting for the wash to finish, then an early night: bed at 9pm, since Mark had to be up at 3am to start work.

Monday 7 September — a night shift and Telford Town Park

Mark up at 2:50am for work. A slow, peaceful shift.

Sasha woke around 6am, came out for hugs, tried to go back to sleep — didn't quite work. She was up properly by 7 for breakfast together, then did her own thing beside Mark while he kept working. Verdict on working through the night: peaceful, but tiring — logging this at half seven in the evening and completely exhausted.

Tried to taxi down to Ironbridge on the extended lunch break — no taxi showed up, and with Sasha's conference registration in the late afternoon, the timing was too risky anyway. Pivoted to Telford Town Park instead: headed in, then remembered a coffee promise and doubled back to the mall for Coffee #1 (a bit weak, but it did the job) and a lemon drizzle cake (a touch sweet, still good). Back into the park after that for a proper walk along the Heritage Trail — nice and peaceful. Spotted one of the big old industrial chimneys (name forgotten), two very cute bunnies, and some squirrels.

Cooking started around 6:30pm — salmon, spinach, frozen pasta, and the rest of the broccoli and cherry tomatoes. Good food. A bit more work afterward to wrap things up, then a grocery run at Aldi.

Back home, Sasha got really, really nervous about the conference — funny, given she's been to plenty before and even presented at some. This time she's just a participant, and it's a buffet crowd sort of thing. Mark walked her over to the conference hall, then came back and cooked a solo dinner: meatballs, asparagus, a Mediterranean salad mix, rice, and the leftover BBQ from Saturday. Made about two and a half portions, only got through one — the rest is in the fridge for another day. Evening wound down quietly after that: a little work, mostly scrolling Instagram.

Then it was time to go meet Sasha halfway as the conference day wrapped up. A bit of a scare on the way: five masked dirt bikes went vrooming past, fast. Made it back safely, hearts a little raised. Then, walking together, they accidentally split a group of three people on the path — said sorry and carried on. A few metres later the group called out, and with hearts already pounding from the dirt bikes, braced for annoyance — but it was: "Excuse me! You two are a beautiful couple." Said thanks and waddled off, relieved.

Back home and showered — Sasha stood inside the portable dryer to dry her hair, resourceful as ever. Signing off at 9:07pm, thoroughly exhausted, with another 2:30am start looming tomorrow.

Tuesday 8 September — Jamaica Coffee Blue, tray bake, and a Wenlock plan that didn't happen

Up at 2:30am for work, expecting to present at 3 — then the meeting got shifted, so there was time for procrastinating and breakfast instead. Properly started work around 4am. Sasha up at 7.

Breakfast was at Jamaica Coffee Blue — Mark got a big breakfast, and Sasha had a tortilla stack: chicken, bell peppers, cheese, béchamel, tortilla, ham, all layered up and topped with crispy cheese. Both dishes were super, super yummy, the tortilla stack especially.

Sasha headed off to the conference, Mark back to work. She came back in the afternoon for lunch — a tray bake at home, everything baked together, easy to make and decent to eat — then it was back out to the conference while Mark kept working.

Sasha had the night off, so the plan was dinner at The Huntsman of Little Wenlock — but no taxi showed up, again. Pivoted to a walk to Sainsbury's for groceries instead, freezing the whole way, with a "Pacific cookie" picked up en route as consolation.

Cooked at home instead: a 1-inch ribeye steak, pasta with spinach, asparagus, and sun-dried tomatoes. Delicious — the kitchen got properly smelly from the steak afterward, but cooking it was genuinely fun. Washed some clothes, then bed.

Wednesday 9 September — an Airwallex interview, Willow lunch, and a cold-waiting mishap

Up at a genuinely normal 6am for an interview with Airwallex. Ate beforehand, so no shared breakfast today. Sasha up at 7, out at 7:45 once the interview wrapped.

Sasha didn't want to go out to the conference again today — fair enough, third day running — but headed back out anyway while Mark kept working. Around 10:30am, Mark had an interview with Binance — went really well, chatting with Dennis (a fellow CUHK alum). Melody from Binance HR called in the afternoon too — overall, looking quite positive.

They met at Willow for lunch, and it was really, really good: a garlic pizza and a chicken kebab flatbread, both super delicious. Good enough that the plan immediately became to go back to Willow again on Thursday.

Mark suddenly had a lot of work pile up and forgot to text Sasha an update — she ended up waiting outside in the cold with no word. She got mad. Rightfully so.

Back together after that, then straight into packing — leaving Telford Thursday morning. Sasha really didn't want to go to the event's proper dinner that evening — reluctant, sad, buried in her own work — so Mark basically had to kick her out the door to go.

While she was out, Mark went to Asda: forgot the recycling bag Sasha had specifically asked about, ended up buying a plastic bag anyway. The haul included pomegranate seeds, chips, and the GU pudding — the lemon cheesecake one this time, after loving the Victoria sponge GU pudding on both Tuesday night and Wednesday afternoon. The lemon one: also super good. Came home and polished off basically all the leftovers, about four portions in one sitting.

Sasha's dinner at the event turned out properly nice: duck meatballs with a cashew hoisin sauce salad to start, then potato mash/gratin, chicken breast, bean sprouts and carrots for the main, and raspberry cheesecake for dessert. She said she'd be leaving around 10pm, and Mark went out to meet her. Finished packing and the last of the laundry after that, since check-out was 10am the next morning — bed around midnight, too late by then to wash Sasha's hair.

Thursday 10 September — leaving Telford, luggage up to Jamaica Coffee Blue

Up around 7am to shower, dry the towels, and pack everything away.

Tried to eat through the fridge before leaving — the lemon cheesecake GU pudding for breakfast, plus some granola, which was really good. Mark headed out around 9am, finished the last of the packing, then checked out of the Airbnb. Overall verdict on the Telford stay: pretty pleasant. A couple of insects turned up in the toilet at points, which made it into the review for the host, but otherwise genuinely nice.

Then came the job of dragging all the luggage — broken wheel and all — back up to Jamaica Coffee Blue, sweating by the time he got there; the rocky path really doesn't get along with a bad suitcase wheel. Made it though — the plan was to hang out and work there while Sasha wrapped up her final conference session, before heading to Birkenhead together.

The last leg of the train toward Birkenhead stopped — someone had gone down on the railway. Sasha's uncle and Heidi came to pick them up from the old station and drove them the rest of the way. Aunt and Gaga were waiting. Unpacked, sat around, then dinner: roast chicken and borscht soup, which felt great after so many days of eating out.

Friday 11 September — Chester, a Pret surcharge, Burger King by the water

Meant to get up early; slept in instead. Up around 8:30 or 9.

Breakfast with Sasha's aunt — the cheese bread was really, really good — then a bit of sitting around before heading for Chester. Bus to Birkenhead Central, train all the way there, and only then the realisation they could have started from Birkenhead Park. Saved that for the way back.

From Chester station they walked into the centre and found the Eastgate Clock. Old buildings, properly historical, quite nice. They didn't feel very safe walking, and the weather was gloomy; that's okay. A loop through town, into the cathedral — really nice, even if the full jaw-on-the-floor cathedral might actually have been Liverpool. Sasha kept dropping her jaw anyway, which was kind of funny. Chocolates, then two sandwiches from Pret, eaten in, with a 20% extra surcharge for dining in (VAT). Sasha was sad the whole time about how expensive it was. The food was good.

Back toward Birkenhead for a 3–3:30 promise to Sasha's aunt. Tried to change the ticket from Birkenhead Central to Birkenhead Park. The clerk was very slow and confused, treating it like they'd used a return in the wrong order, asking where the outbound ticket was, calling it "really weird," then saying it wasn't a problem. No extra to pay — same price — and on the actual train into Birkenhead Park they didn't even need the new ticket.

Gaga got home from school. Burger King on the Birkenhead seaside, by Morrisons: a table of four and a two, five people, a bit awkward, Heidi in the little corner. Shared three burgers and twenty nuggets. Very happy. Sasha's uncle and the other family were coming over — aunt and two cousins spent the whole time looking out for them, while Gaga kept leaning toward Sasha's aunt so she wouldn't have to sit close to the cousins. Afterward a seaside walk with the cousins, peaceful and kind of windy. The younger boy cousin: just very weird. Oh well. Met aunt and uncle in Morrisons for a bit of shopping, Oreo ice cream in the car watching the sunset, then Marks & Spencer for a little more food, home because it was getting late. Good thing they weren't starving after Burger King.

Dinner in: seafood pasta with cheese, salmon, and purple broccolini, with Toy Story 5 (?) on in the background.

Saturday 12 September — a long wash, Costco, and a barbecue

Up relatively early. The washing machine was supposed to take 53 minutes and took about an hour 53.

Breakfast was cheese bread and ham — quite nice — then over to Sasha's uncle's to pick him up, and a drive together to a Chinese supermarket in Liverpool. Funny mix: some things crazy expensive, others reasonably priced, and a bag of bean sprouts as big as a torso. Then Costco, walking around everywhere, Sasha's cousin Kaka pushing all three of them on a cart. Really fun. Food there was pretty cheap: an 18-inch pizza for £10, of which they only ate half and brought the rest back, plus hot dogs and baked potatoes. Aldi after that, before the barbecue.

Back to Sasha's aunt's for a bit of packing and a rest. Around 5pm they headed to Sasha's uncle's. Funny from the door: the younger cousin was on his Nintendo Switch and didn't say hi to anyone. Sasha got really mad — hello, who are you saying hello to? Why won't you look at us when you talk? — while Mark and the other two cousins laughed in the kitchen and Sasha shouted at him from the living room.

Relatively chill: chatting, looking at old pictures, uncle cooking sausages, chicken wings, a huge steak. Highlight of the night was roasting marshmallows on the barbecue in the backyard. Funny because Sasha's uncle was trying to barbecue some bread — whenever he moved it away, everyone's marshmallows went for his space. The bread was really big, and there wasn't much fire, but it was fun. Gaga's marshmallow went completely black; she said that was intentional. Heidi and Sasha were very patient and turned out really brown, really big marshmallows that looked yummy. Two dogs, 阿肥 and 阿福 — one big, one very pretty. 阿肥 looks like Oyo, really cute. They were fighting too. Left around 11pm: a good six hours of barbecue.

Sunday 13 September — Liverpool ONE, a jaw-drop cathedral, The Batman

Mark up at 7:30 to watch the boats, then straight back to sleep. Out around 8:30 or 9, over to Liverpool just to see the city.

Liverpool ONE was a very nice, modern mall — not the old-style Telford centre. Dunkin' Donuts first: so sweet, so expensive, about £4 for a single donut. Then Greggs for a sausage roll, a little oilier than ideal, still very yummy. Auntie Anne's sugary churro pretzels, super delicious after brushing off about 80% of the sugar. Into LEGO: a Lando Norris helmet and an Oscar Piastri helmet — thinking of getting those back in Hong Kong. In and out of shops after that.

A sandwich from a random store — decent filling, really dry bread, good taste — then Chinatown for the shade, lucky it was so sunny. Liverpool Cathedral was the one where Sasha's jaw kept dropping. Huge, really, really nice. (They heard fifth-largest in the world, longest in the UK — for the book: it is the longest cathedral in the world, and the largest in Britain.)

Back through Liverpool ONE for Black Sheep Coffee, on Gaga's recommendation. Pretty good. The shop was a bit hot, and Sasha's cramps were coming on, which wasn't great. Home to Birkenhead around four — Sunday, everything closing early, and they wanted to be ready for the next day.

They actually got some work done, first real chance in days. Sasha followed her aunt making bánh mì and got to eat a little; aunt had also made multi-grain raisin bread while they were out. Dinner in front of The Batman (Robert Pattinson, 2022): leftover barbecue chicken wings, pizza because the white bread was mouldy, asparagus, pan bacon on mushrooms with cheese, and aunt's Portuguese shrimp with lemon, vinegar, onion and garlic — very yummy. The film was very dark, very quiet, decent, about three hours, done around midnight. Then the usual living-room chat — Heidi sometimes, Kaka sometimes (usually asleep on the sofa by twelve). Very relaxing. Packed up, bed.

Wednesday 16 September — Rabbies, a Highland coo, and Loch Ness

Up at 6:20, out by 7:20. Earlier Edinburgh days still to fill in.

Breakfast at the hotel was pancakes and a smoked salmon bagel — they ate a lot, having read there wouldn't be food on the tour. Outside, the morning air was freezing, and something like sunrise sat over Queen Street — not the actual sunrise, but beautiful. A twenty-minute walk to the bus station, then onto the coach with Louise, a lovely, talkative South African driver who spent the day on folklore, history, and her own stories. Behind and in front were two middle-aged Chinese couples; the ones behind were a bit inconsiderate — loud, and eating smelly food.

Out of Edinburgh they passed a beautiful school that costs something like £20–30k a year, then one of the three Forth bridges — three spans from three different centuries — and then the countryside: sheep, cows, Louise still talking. First stop was a toilet break with jam shopping and a Highland coo to feed. The tongue was huge, wet, sticky, and warm, and it gobbled the snacks right out of the hand. Hands got washed with soap four times afterward. The coos were very cute.

A quick drop at the Welcome to the Highlands sign for photos, then a lot of sleeping on the bus, scenery most of the way. Loch Ness was the main event. The cruise was so zen — front-left seats, lots of water to look at, quite chilly. The town is all about the monster, which apparently hasn't been seen in 1,500 years, though people (and even government money) still keep looking. Back on the bus, Louise handed out whisky and teacakes. Whisky is not their cup of tea; the teacakes were super sweet. Too much sugar in the day already.

The way home took a slightly different path and stopped at three mountains called Faith, Hope and Charity — the view was breathtaking. Loch means lake, and it has a lokhhh sound. They got off to drink fresh stream water: it tasted somehow boiled, not too minerally, a tad sweet. Snacks on board: four chocolate chip cookies, two muffins, a bread, and a protein bar. Some of the history overlapped with Kieran's castle tour the day before. All well.

Lucky with the weather: sunny and clear to start, white clouds, then overcast, but it never really rained. The Highlands in different lights. A last stop for a cheeseburger and souvenirs — keychains, including one for Ginger's birthday (the little cat who passed a day before his birthday this year; it had been seven weeks last Sunday), and some whisky. Back in Edinburgh they wandered Queen Street and George Street, then reheated M&S fridge meals bought the day before: mushroom risotto, tomato basil pasta with chicken, blueberries and tangerine.

After a shower, a call with Melody from Binance HR. The job didn't come through — notice period and non-compete. Quite sad, somewhat expected. She said they wanted to hire him and that he did really well; it came down to the notice and the clause. Early night: 7am train to London in the morning.
